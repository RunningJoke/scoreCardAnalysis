Rally Impact Model

How much does a player change the chance that her team wins a rally, once teammates, opponents and who serves are accounted for? This canvas describes an algorithm that answers that from SAMS score sheets alone, and turns the answer into player profiles for scouting, line-up decisions and form tracking.

Step 0 · Input

What a score sheet gives us

Recorded

  • Starting six per set in rotation order I–VI
  • Substitutions with the score at the change
  • Service rounds: the score at which each server's turn ended. These rebuild every rally in order.
  • Timeouts, set scores, set times, home and away
  • Roster with the used players, captain, liberos and MVP

Not recorded

  • Who scored or lost a point: no attack, block, reception or error stats
  • Libero changes
  • Playing positions (setter, opposite, middle, outside)

So the only individual signal is the server. Everything else has to be inferred from who was on court when rallies were won or lost. About 180 rallies per match make that workable.

Steps 1–4 · Data preparation

Turning sheets into a rally table

1

Parse

In
One SAMS PDF (text layer)
Out
Match record: teams, home/away, date, league, and per set the start six, subs, service-round end scores and timeouts; roster as number → name with libero and captain flags
  • Substitution scores are written from the substituting team's side. This was checked on the four LL4 2026/27 sheets.
  • Fix the text encoding on read (umlauts come out as K�kten in the raw text layer).
2

Rebuild rallies

Walk each set from 0:0. The serving team keeps serving until the score in its service-round entry is reached, and then the other team wins the next point (a sideout) and rotates. Substitutions are applied at their recorded score. Each rally gets both on-court sixes, the server and the winner.

3

Validate

A set enters the model only if all of these hold. Otherwise it is logged and left out, so one bad parse can't distort a rating.

  • The rebuilt final score equals the printed set score
  • Every substitution score lies on the rebuilt score path
  • At most 6 substitutions per team per set; every number is on the roster
  • The number of service rounds matches the number of sideouts
4

Identify players

Jersey numbers are only valid within one match. A player ID is the normalized name (Unicode NFC, case-folded, "Surname, Firstname"). The same name on another team or in another season is treated as the same person, which carries information across promotion, relegation and transfers.

  • Near-matches (one letter off, double names) go to a manual review list instead of being merged silently
  • Liberos are marked and are not rated individually (see the team term)
Step 5 · Core model

Regularized adjusted plus-minus, per rally

Every rally is framed the same way: does the serving team win this point (a break point)? The probability depends on the twelve players on court, the server and both teams' baselines. This is basketball's RAPM, applied to rallies instead of possessions.

logit P(serving team wins rally r) =

  + σserver(r)  + τserving team − τreceiving team

θiOverall impact of player i while on courtPrior rating (previous window)λθ
δisrv, δircvPhase split: extra impact when her team serves (breaking) or receives (siding out)0λδ > λθ
σkServe rating: extra break chance on rallies player k serves herselfPrior ratingλσ
τtTeam and libero term: everything constant for a team that the listed six don't explain (libero, system, coaching)0λτ ≫ λθ

Fitting

  • Minimize the weighted log-loss over all rallies in the window plus the ridge penalties: λθΣ(θ − θprior)² + λδΣδ² + λσΣ(σ − σprior)² + λτΣτ²
  • Choose the λ values by forward-chaining cross-validation: fit up to match day d, score the rallies of day d+1
  • λτ is kept large so team strength is credited to players wherever the data allows. τ only absorbs what can't be attributed, mainly the libero.
  • Players who always play together can't be told apart. The ridge then splits their credit evenly instead of producing extreme values.

Units people can read

  • Impact = point difference per 100 rallies compared with an average league player: 100 · 2 · (P(β+θ) − P(β)), roughly 50·θ. This is the same unit as the v0 squad report.
  • Serve = extra break points per 100 own serves: 100 · (P(β+σ) − P(β))
  • Sideout and break impact = θ + δrcv and θ + δsrv, in the same unit

Leverage weighting (optional)

Each rally gets a weight equal to how much it moves the chance of winning the set: L(r) = P(set | point won) − P(set | point lost). This comes from a set-level Markov chain that uses the league sideout and break rates and plays to 25 (15 in a fifth set), win by 2. Weights are normalized to a mean of 1.

Clutch = θ fitted with leverage weights − θ fitted without them. A rally at 23:23 counts about three times as much as one at 5:12.

Windows: season and form

The same model is fitted twice per match day d:

  • Season: all rallies up to d. The prior is last season's rating × ρ (for example 0.7), or 0 for a new player.
  • Form: only the last K matches (for example 3), shrunk toward the season rating from before the window. Form delta = form − season.
Step 6 · Trust

How sure is each number?

Bootstrap by match

Resample whole matches within the window about 200 times and refit. The 10th to 90th percentile of the results is the 80 % band shown next to every rating. Resampling whole matches keeps the rallies of one match together, so the band isn't falsely narrow.

Separability and sample flags

  • Inseparable: her on-court pattern correlates above 0.95 with a teammate's. The rating is effectively shared and the profile says with whom.
  • Small sample: fewer than 100 rallies in the window. The rating is shown greyed out.
  • Libero: no individual rating. Her contribution is inside the team term τ.

Modules on top of the profiles

What the ratings are used for

Scouting an opponent

For: the next game's game plan
  • Players ranked by impact, with the band and flags
  • Best and weakest servers (σ)
  • Rotation table: sideout % and break % in each of their 6 rotations, observed vs expected. The weakest sideout rotation is where to serve hardest.
  • Their usual starting six and substitution habits (who comes on when trailing)

Our own line-up

For: who starts, who comes on
  • Line-up predictor: pick any six and an opponent. The model gives sideout % and break %, and the Markov chain turns them into P(set win).
  • Compare candidates side by side, e.g. "#12 instead of #18 against Team C: +6 % set-win chance"
  • Our own rotation table, to place strong servers and passers

Form over time

For: spotting slumps and development
  • Season and form curves per player, refreshed after every match day
  • An alert when form leaves the season band two match days in a row
  • Team view: whose form moved most since the last report
Does it work?

Backtest before trusting a single rating

Fit on match days 1…d and predict day d+1, then roll forward through the season. Score with log-loss on rallies and accuracy on set winners.

Limits and settings

What it can't do, and what to tune

Known limits

  • It measures a player's effect on rallies, not her actions. A great passer next to a weak hitter can look average.
  • Positions are unknown, so a setter and an outside are compared on one scale.
  • Liberos get no individual rating, and middles may carry a little of the libero's effect.
  • Teams that never substitute give wide bands. Only cross-season and cross-team data can narrow them.
  • Name matching can merge two different people with the same name. These cases go to manual review.
